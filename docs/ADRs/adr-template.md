# ADR-NNN: Short title in the imperative

- **Status:** Proposed | Accepted | Superseded by ADR-NNN
- **Date:** YYYY-MM-DD

## Context

The forces and constraints that make a decision necessary: what problem appeared, what
limits apply (team, time, budget, compliance, existing systems), and what would go wrong if
nothing were decided. Facts, not the conclusion.

## Decision

What was chosen, in the active voice — *"We use X"*, not *"X will be used"*. One decision
per record.

## Consequences

What this makes easy, and what it makes hard. Include the cost honestly; an ADR that lists
only benefits is marketing, and the next maintainer will not trust it.

For a **rejection** ADR — a technology or pattern deliberately turned down — also state the
concrete threshold or event that would make us revisit it (a load figure, a team size, a
failure mode, a compliance requirement). Without that trigger, the rejection is a matter of
taste and will be re-litigated.
